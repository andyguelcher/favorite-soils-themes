SAMPLE DATA: a sample timeline for the Scobey series, so you can try "Play its history" and the timeline editor. Replace it with your own soil's stages. Each stage starts with "Stage:"; the README explains every line.

Stage: An ice sheet leaves till
When: during the last glaciation
Deposit: Ap, Bt, Bk1, Bk2, Bky
Text: A continental ice sheet spreads clay loam till across the plains of northern Montana. That till is the parent material of every horizon in this soil.
Source: Scobey OSD

Stage: Grassland builds a dark topsoil
When: after the ice melts
Develop: Ap
Text: Bluebunch wheatgrass, western wheatgrass and needlegrasses root into the till, and their roots darken the top of the soil with organic matter.
Source: Scobey OSD

Stage: Clay moves down
When: over thousands of years
Develop: Bt
Text: Water seeping down carries fine clay out of the topsoil and leaves it as clay films on the faces of peds in the Bt horizon.
Source: Scobey OSD

Stage: Lime and gypsum gather below
When: over thousands of years, still going on
Finish: Bt, Bk1, Bk2, Bky
Text: With only about 12 inches of precipitation a year, water dissolves lime and gypsum near the surface but soaks only so far, leaving masses of lime in the Bk horizons and gypsum in the Bky.
Source: Scobey OSD

Stage: Plowed for dryland crops
When: today
Finish: all
Text: Cultivation mixes the top 6 inches into an Ap horizon. Scobey soils are used mainly for dryland crops, with some rangeland.
Source: Scobey OSD
